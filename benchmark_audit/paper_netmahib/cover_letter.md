Dear Editors,

Please consider “Batch-dependent negative sampling and marginal-preserving decoy construction in TCR–peptide benchmarks” as an original research article in Network Modeling Analysis in Health Informatics and Bioinformatics.

The study concerns the reliability of computational bioinformatics benchmarks when negative examples are constructed from positive observations. An executable source audit on a frozen VDJdb release shows that batch composition and category-based fallback can change antigen marginals and create learnable label signals. We separate registry exclusion from marginal preservation, formulate a closed assignment using an established optimization solver, measure its retention cost, and test metadata controls with receptor partitions fixed before construction.

The contribution is a reproducible methodological audit and an explicit construction contract. We do not claim a new general assignment algorithm, experimentally validated nonbinding labels, or improved biological binding prediction. The manuscript reports negative and sensitivity results, including complementary-fold effects and loss of HLA coverage. It is relevant to the journal's interest in statistical, graph-theoretic and machine-learning methods for bioinformatics.

The source code, manifests, measurements, prediction records and figures are public at https://github.com/Sharon-codes/Precision-Oncology/tree/a4162ded50ba24d76ab8d5134577eba6935f910b/benchmark_audit. A supplementary methods and results file accompanies the article. We request subscription publication.
